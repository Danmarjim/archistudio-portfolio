In Italy, an architect for a home renovation usually costs between 5% and 15% of the building cost, with 7–12% typical for a full apartment renovation, according to industry guides consulted in October 2026. Alternatively the fee can be fixed, split into stages, or hourly for consultations. Fees are unregulated: each professional calculates them differently, which is exactly why quotes are hard to compare.

In this guide I explain how fees are calculated, what you pay for at each stage, whether you can hire an architect for just part of the job and how to compare two quotes.

## How an architect's renovation fee is calculated

Since 2012 Italy has had no minimum professional tariffs: fees are agreed freely with the client. Ministerial Decree 140/2012 sets parameters, but they mainly serve as a reference when a court has to settle a fee, not as a price list. In practice there are three methods:

| Method | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Percentage of the works | A share of the building cost, usually 5–15% | Scales with the size of the job | You only know the final cost once the works are quoted |
| Fixed fee | A set amount, often split into stages | You know from the start what you'll spend | Each stage's scope must be clearly defined |
| Hourly | An hourly rate, usually €50–150 | Suits consultations and short jobs | Hard to predict on a long project |

Sources: [Spazi Belli](https://www.spazibelli.com/consigli/quanto-costa-un-architetto-per-ristrutturare-casa), [Vitae Studio](https://vitaestudioarchitettura.it/blog/quanto-costa-un-architetto/), [CalcolaParcella](https://www.calcolaparcella.it/blog/quanto-costa-architetto-ristrutturazione), October 2026. As an example, for €50,000 of works the same sources give a fee of €2,500–5,000.

## What you pay for: the stages of the work

A fee isn't an abstract number: behind it are specific stages, each with something tangible you receive.

1. **Survey and checks.** Measurements and photos of every room, 2D drawings of the current state and, if needed, a request for the municipal records to check the home is compliant.
2. **Design.** Concept (layout and furnished plans), developed design (style, materials and colour moodboard, 3D views) and technical design (detailed drawings for tendering, drawings for bespoke furniture).
3. **Design supervision.** Bill of quantities, finding and comparing contractors, choosing materials and finishes, checking the aesthetic result on site.
4. **Site supervision.** Schedule, site visits and meetings, checking progress valuations before each payment.
5. **Permits and land registry.** Building permit filed with the municipality under the signing professional's responsibility, completion notice and land registry update.

## Can you hire an architect for just part of the job?

Yes, and it often makes sense. You can ask for:

- **just the design**, if you already have a trusted contractor and want to manage the site yourself;
- **just the permits**, if the design is simple but you need a professional to sign;
- **just design supervision**, if you want someone to choose materials and finishes with you and check the aesthetic result.

In my work each stage can be hired on its own: many clients start with the design and decide later whether to continue with site supervision.

## Is the fee tax-deductible?

In Italy, when the works qualify for a building tax incentive, the related professional fees (design, site supervision, permits) usually follow the same deduction. Rates, caps and requirements change almost every year, though: check the rules in force in the year of the works and ask for the invoice to describe the services correctly. When I supervise a site, I help set up invoices and paperwork.

## How to compare two architects' quotes

Two quotes with different figures often describe different work. Before choosing, check:

- which stages are included and which aren't;
- how many site visits are planned;
- what you actually receive: drawings, 3D views, bill of quantities, bespoke furniture drawings;
- which costs are extra: municipal fees, land registry, any structural engineering;
- whether the professional is registered and has professional indemnity insurance.

## When it's worth it (and when it isn't)

An architect is worth it when you change the layout, replace the services, need permits or want a result designed down to the detail: a good design avoids mistakes that cost far more than the fee. If you only want to repaint or choose new furniture, a [60-minute consultation](/servicios/consulenza-architetto-online) or a [restyling](/servicios/restyling-casa) is enough.

**Do I pay everything up front?**
Usually not: the fee is split into payments tied to the stages of the work. [DA CONFERMARE: how you structure payments]

**Architect or surveyor (geometra)?**
In Italy, for many apartment works both can sign the permits. The difference is training: an architect is trained to design spaces, light and interiors as well as the technical side.

**Does an interior architect cost more?**
Not necessarily: it depends on the scope. An interior design with lots of bespoke furniture needs more drawings and time than one using catalogue furniture.
