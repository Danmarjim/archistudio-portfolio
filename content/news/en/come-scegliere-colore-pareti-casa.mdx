Choosing wall colours for your home takes five steps: **look at the light** in the room, **consider its proportions**, **decide the balance between colours**, **test a large sample** and **judge it next to the floor and furniture**. The fear of getting it wrong almost always comes from skipping the last step and choosing from a fan deck in a shop.

In this guide I explain the method I use on my projects, with palettes from real homes. If you're interested in the why before the how, I've written an article on [colour as a design tool](/news/il-colore-nell-architettura).

## How to choose wall colours: start from the light

The same colour changes a lot depending on which way a room faces:

| Orientation | What the light is like | What works |
|---|---|---|
| North | Cool and steady | Warm, light tones: ivory, sand, dusty pink. Avoid cool greys |
| South | Warm and strong | You can go darker or cooler: greens, blues, greys |
| East | Warm in the morning, cool in the evening | Colours that cope with the change: warm neutrals, soft greens |
| West | Cool in the morning, warm and golden in the evening | Fresh tones that warm up in the evening: dusty blues, sage |

Artificial light matters too: a warm bulb (2,700–3,000 K) warms and yellows a colour; a cooler one (4,000 K) cools it. Test the colour with the lights you'll actually use.

## Room size and proportions

- In a **small or dark room**, a light, warm colour opens it up and brightens it; but a dark colour on every wall can also make it cosy and enveloping, if that's the intention.
- A **high ceiling** comes down with a colour darker than the walls, or with a band of colour dropping 30–40 cm from the ceiling.
- A **long corridor** feels shorter if you paint the end wall.
- A **low ceiling** feels higher if you carry the walls' light colour onto it.

## Combining colours: the 60-30-10 rule (and when to break it)

The 60-30-10 rule says a room feels balanced when about **60%** is a dominant colour (usually the walls), **30%** a secondary colour (floor, large furniture, textiles) and **10%** an accent colour (objects, one wall, a detail).

At [CASA PEONIA](/proyectos/restyling-casa-peonia) the living area works exactly like this:

- **60%**: the pale ivory walls, which replaced electric blue and brighten the whole floor;
- **30%**: the light oak floor and the near-black brown of the entrance wall, which turns into the living room;
- **10%**: the gold yellow of the velvet sofa and of the semicircles painted on the wall.

The rule is a starting point, not an obligation. In a room you want to feel enveloping, like the blue [ITALIAN SUMMER](/proyectos/bagno-italian-summer) bathroom, one colour can reach 80%. For combinations there are two safe routes: **analogous colours** (close on the colour wheel, like green and blue) for a calm effect, and **complementary colours** (opposites, like blue and orange) for a lively one, using the second only as an accent.

## Walls, ceilings and half walls

Colour doesn't have to cover a whole wall:

- **half walls**: in the [PARIGINA Kitchen](/proyectos/cucina-parigina) the floor tiles run up the walls to half height, protecting them and shaping the room;
- **painted shapes**: at CASA PEONIA, gold-yellow semicircles frame a Sardinian rug and a ceramic sun, like a backdrop for the objects in the home;
- **colour that turns the corner**: a coloured wall that carries on round the corner links two spaces;
- **coloured ceilings**: in a bedroom, a painted ceiling makes the space feel more enclosed.

## How to test a sample (properly)

A small sample tells you almost nothing. To test it properly:

1. paint a large sample, **at least 50×50 cm**, or a piece of card you can move around;
2. apply **two coats**, as on the real wall, and wait for it to dry: wet paint looks darker;
3. try it on **two different walls**, one in light and one in shade;
4. look at it **in the morning, the afternoon and the evening** under artificial light;
5. judge it **next to the floor, furniture and fabrics** that are staying.

## If you're renting

In Italy, tenants must return a home in the condition they received it, apart from normal wear and tear (art. 1590 of the Italian Civil Code). So before painting:

- **ask the landlord's permission**, ideally in writing;
- choose colours that are **easy to cover** if you'll need to repaint at the end;
- or bring in colour with things you can take with you: **removable wallpaper**, curtains, cushions, pictures and a [rug](/tappeti) that changes a whole room.

## The most common mistakes

- **Choosing from the fan deck in the shop**, under light that isn't your home's.
- **Ignoring the floor**: warm parquet or a grey floor changes every colour next to it.
- **Too many different colours** with no dominant one.
- **All white "to be safe"**: white isn't neutral and, used everywhere, it flattens rooms. I explain why in the article on [colour in interior architecture](/news/il-colore-nell-architettura).
- **Deciding the colour last**, when furniture and floors are already chosen and there's little room left to manoeuvre.
